# Product principles

## Product outcome

Create a small set of high-quality interactions that a child can understand without reading, use briefly with an adult nearby, and connect to real objects and conversation.

The product is not a passive video library and not a general intelligence test. It is a practice environment for observable component skills.

## Learning model

Each activity should satisfy as many of these conditions as possible:

- **Active:** the child makes a meaningful decision, not merely taps to trigger animation.
- **Engaged:** the interface holds attention without unrelated motion or rewards.
- **Meaningful:** content maps to familiar objects, routines, language and quantities.
- **Social:** the adult can ask, model, wait and extend the task.
- **Goal-aligned:** every interaction is traceable to one primary learning objective.

## Development model

- Initial content is selected by broad age band.
- Advancement is based on demonstrated mastery and tolerance, not birthday alone.
- One major challenge dimension changes at a time.
- Accuracy, explanation and strategy matter more than speed.
- A child may move forward in one domain and remain at an earlier level in another.
- Regression after a break is normal; review is scheduled without calling it failure.

## Experience model

- One task per screen.
- Demonstrate before asking.
- Offer 2–3 choices for the youngest band.
- Use large tap/drag targets and forgiving hit areas.
- Pair audio with visual cues.
- Do not rely on color alone.
- Keep animation purposeful, interruptible and reducible.
- Use calm, specific feedback: “Ai găsit aceeași formă,” not generic confetti.
- End while the child is still regulated; never require “one more level.”

## Ethical model

- No dark patterns or retention mechanics.
- No behavioral advertising or tracking.
- No comparison with other children.
- No score interpreted as intelligence, maturity or diagnosis.
- No child profile in the cloud.
- Parent can inspect, export and delete all local data.
- Data minimization is preferred over consent flows.

## Success metrics

Product success is not daily active use. Preferred measures are local and parent-visible:

- child understands the mechanic with fewer demonstrations;
- accuracy and strategy improve on the same or closely related tasks;
- hints decline without rising frustration;
- child can perform a related activity with real objects;
- session ends voluntarily and calmly;
- parent reports useful conversation or play after the activity.

These observations should be descriptive, not normative or diagnostic.
