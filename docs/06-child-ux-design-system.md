# Child UX, audio and motion design

## Core screen rule

One objective per screen. The child should know where to look and what to do from a short demonstration, not from text.

## Touch and drag

Platform minimums are roughly 44 pt on iOS and 48 dp on Android. For 30–36 months, use substantially larger targets:

- primary targets: 72–96 logical pixels where layout permits;
- minimum hit area: 64 × 64 for toddler gameplay;
- generous invisible hit slop;
- 16–24 spacing between unrelated targets;
- snap radius larger than the visible drop zone;
- no precision dragging near screen edges.

Motor difficulty must not contaminate a memory or logic task. When the goal is classification, accept approximate drops.

## Visual hierarchy

- one focal object or instruction cue;
- 2–3 answer options for the youngest band;
- simple backgrounds;
- restrained palette;
- shape/icon plus color;
- no decorative buttons near answers;
- consistent placement for replay, pause and parent exit;
- child exit uses a neutral home icon, not text.

Avoid:

- rapid scene changes;
- constantly moving background elements;
- flashing;
- tiny badges;
- visual clutter;
- confetti that hides the next action;
- mascot speech longer than the task.

## Demonstration pattern

1. Focus target appears.
2. Hand/arrow demonstrates one action.
3. Audio says one short phrase.
4. Demonstration disappears or becomes replayable.
5. Child receives control.

Never repeat the demonstration automatically in a loop. Provide a large replay button.

## Feedback language

Preferred:

- “Ai găsit aceeași formă.”
- “Ai pus lucrurile roșii împreună.”
- “Mai uită-te la model.”
- “Încearcă mai întâi piesa mare.”
- “Putem opri aici.”

Avoid:

- “Genial!”
- “Ești cel mai deștept!”
- “Greșit.”
- “Ai pierdut.”
- “Mai ai o singură viață.”
- comparative praise.

Praise strategy or effort briefly, not identity.

## Audio

- Romanian-first.
- Human narration preferred over unstable text-to-speech.
- Short phrases, moderate pace and low emotional intensity.
- All critical audio has a visual equivalent.
- Replay is always available.
- Music off by default or very low, independently controllable.
- No sudden sound effects.
- No microphone or speech recognition.

Example instruction lengths:

- 30–36m: “Pune cercul aici.”
- 3–4y: “Găsește animalul galben.”
- 4–5y: “Acum sortăm după formă.”
- 5–6y: “Ține minte ordinea și repet-o.”

## Motion

Motion must explain state:

- object follows the finger;
- target gently highlights;
- correct placement settles;
- rule switch visibly changes cue;
- path shows progress.

Motion must not manufacture excitement.

Guidelines:

- common transitions approximately 180–350 ms;
- avoid long bouncing or repeated pulsing;
- respect Reduce Motion;
- no critical timing encoded only in animation;
- child can interrupt or pause;
- no flashing or rapid peripheral movement.

## Accessibility

- VoiceOver/TalkBack labels for every actionable object.
- Logical traversal order.
- Do not rely on audio, color or gesture alone.
- Haptics can supplement, never replace, visual feedback.
- Parent can enable high contrast, reduced motion, no music, slower demonstration and larger targets.
- Support screen orientation selected for the game; avoid frequent rotation.
- Test with audio off and with one-handed parent assistance.

## Character/mascot

A mascot may:

- demonstrate;
- wait;
- point to the current goal;
- celebrate briefly;
- suggest a break.

It must not:

- beg the child to return;
- look sad when the app closes;
- create a daily obligation;
- offer random rewards;
- obstruct the task;
- speak continuously.

## End of session

Use a predictable ending:

1. final easy or familiar action;
2. brief strategy feedback;
3. optional offline mission;
4. calm closing screen;
5. device can be handed back to adult.

No “continue” button in the child area after the limit. The adult can start another session only through parent mode.
