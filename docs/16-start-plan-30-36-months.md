# Start plan for 30–36 months

This is the safest initial product configuration for a child around 31 months. It is not a developmental test and does not require daily use.

## Initial profile

```json
{
  "ageBand": "A30_36",
  "sessionMinutes": 5,
  "audioEnabled": true,
  "musicEnabled": false,
  "hapticsEnabled": false,
  "reducedMotion": true,
  "coPlayPrompts": true,
  "startingChoiceCount": 2
}
```

The real app may store an optional local display name. The repository and telemetry must not contain it.

## First activity order

Introduce one mechanic at a time. Do not unlock by calendar date; unlock after calm, repeated understanding.

1. `same-picture` — tap one of two very different choices.
2. `sort-by-color` — place two to four objects in two large bins.
3. `inset-puzzle` — fit two large pieces without rotation.
4. `daily-order` — put two familiar routine cards in order.
5. `one-to-one-count` — give one object to each character, up to three.
6. `shadow-match` — match familiar outlines with two choices.
7. `peek-and-find` — remember one object under two locations, no delay.
8. `wait-for-go` — wait for a clear signal; very few trials.
9. `listen-find` — one short audio instruction with two pictures.
10. `simple-maze` — wide path, no hazard and one branch at most.
11. `emotion-match` — match one obvious facial cue to a simple context.
12. `real-color-hunt` — phone gives the prompt, then attention moves into the room.

## A single session

- one familiar warm-up;
- one current learning task;
- optionally one real-world prompt;
- total around 3–5 minutes at first;
- end sooner if attention shifts or frustration appears.

The adult may say:

- “Uită-te la model.”
- “Unde este la fel?”
- “Ce punem mai întâi?”
- “Putem opri aici.”

Avoid rapid correction or identity praise. Give time, then one useful cue. A wrong tap should not trigger a harsh sound or forced repetition.

## Move forward when

- the action is understood without repeated demonstration;
- three recent attempts are completed calmly and cleanly;
- no hint is needed;
- the skill appears across different pictures or themes.

Change only one variable: for example two choices → three choices. Do not also hide the model and increase distractor similarity.

## Hold or simplify when

- motor effort is causing the errors;
- the child repeatedly taps decorative elements;
- two errors occur in succession;
- the adult must explain more than the app demonstration;
- the child turns away, pushes the phone away or becomes upset.

These are usability/support signals, not evidence of low ability.
